# Belize — Data Protection Act, 2021

A profile set that finds Belizean personal data in a database and masks it, built for the
Data Protection Act, 2021 (Act No. 45 of 2021, gazetted 30 November 2021). The Act comes into force
on a day the Minister appoints by Order (s. 97); no commencement Order was found in the public sources
consulted in September 2026, so confirm its status before relying on a date. Data from which no
individual can be identified — even with other information the controller holds — is not personal
data (s. 2). The goal is a non-production copy that no longer identifies anyone: not only the direct
identifiers, but the quasi-identifiers that re-identify people in combination, the sensitive personal
data of section 2 — which in Belize includes **financial record or position** and **criminal
proceedings** — and health records.

## Detection

Every domain has a **Path** classifier on the column name, in English, in the abbreviations of
Belizean systems (`SSN`, `SSB_NO`, `TIN`, `BHIS_NO`, `NHI_NO`, `DOB`) and in Spanish. Names that are
ambiguous on their own (`NAME`, `RESULT`, `CITY`, `PARTY`, `TENURE`) carry a lower strength and need the
values to agree; generic ones exclude what they are not (`PRODUCT_NAME`, `MESSAGE_ID`, `PARTY_ID`).

Where the values have a recognizable shape or vocabulary, a **Regex** or **List** classifier finds
the data in columns whose name says nothing (`FIELD1`, `REMARKS`): social security numbers, given
names and surnames, e-mail, Belizean phone numbers, addresses with a street or a mile marker, cards
(Luhn), IPs, MAC and IMEI, plates with their district code, VIN, land parcels by block and parcel,
towns and villages, ethnic groups, languages, ICD-10 codes, medicines, blood groups, religions,
parties, trade unions, social security benefits and social programmes.

**Type** classifiers rule domains out where the column cannot hold the data: an e-mail is text of at
least 5 characters, a birth date a date, timestamp or text, an amount a number.

## Masking

All algorithms are deterministic for the engine's key, so the same value masks the same way in
every table and every job. Placeholders such as `N/A` or `-` do not fail a row: where there is
nothing to mask they pass through.

### L1 — Direct identifiers

| Domain | Masking |
|---|---|
| `BZ_L1_SOCIAL_SECURITY_NUMBER` | The nine digits mapped digit by digit, separators kept; one-to-one |
| `BZ_L1_GIVEN_NAME`, `BZ_L1_SURNAME` | Word by word from Belizean given-name and surname lists; a hyphen kept |
| `BZ_L1_FULL_NAME` | Given names and surnames in English order, a middle initial and `SURNAME, GIVEN` too |
| `BZ_L1_EMAIL` | Name and domain mapped character by character, top-level domain `.test` |
| `BZ_L1_PHONE` | `+501` and the first digit (mobile or district) kept, the other six masked |
| `BZ_L1_ADDRESS` | A fictitious Belizean address: street, highway mile marker, or lot and neighbourhood |
| `BZ_L1_PAYMENT_CARD` | Payment Card (Luhn stays valid) |
| `BZ_L1_IP_ADDRESS` | Each IPv4 octet mapped to 1–254 |
| `BZ_L1_LICENCE_PLATE` | District code and category letter kept, serial digits masked |
| `BZ_L1_LAND_PARCEL` | Block and parcel numbers masked, registration section kept |
| `BZ_L1_GEOLOCATION` | Integer part and first decimal kept (about 11 km), the rest masked |
| `BZ_L1_FREE_TEXT` | Free Text Redaction: SSNs, e-mail, phones, cards, plates, IPs, names |
| `BZ_L1_CREDENTIAL` | Every character becomes `X` |
| TIN, passport, voter ID, driver's licence, immigration document, civil registry, bank account, device, VIN, contract, user and address detail domains | Character Mapping: vowels to vowels, consonants to consonants, digits to digits; hex kept hex for devices |

### L2 — Quasi-identifiers

| Domain | Masking |
|---|---|
| `BZ_L2_BIRTH_DATE` | Shifted up to 60 days |
| `BZ_L2_EVENT_DATE` | Death, marriage, hiring, admission, arrest and similar dates, shifted up to 30 days |
| `BZ_L2_BIRTH_YEAR` | The decade stays, the last digit is masked |
| `BZ_L2_AGE` | The decade stays (37 → 3x); 100 and over becomes 90 |
| `BZ_L2_TOWN_VILLAGE` | Places under 5,000 inhabitants (2022 census) become the nearest place of the same district with at least 5,000 |
| `BZ_L2_SEX` | Replaced within its own vocabulary |
| `BZ_L2_DEPENDANTS` | Capped at 5 |
| neighbourhood, nationality, marital status, occupation, employer, education, school | Replaced from a list |

### L3 — Sensitive personal data (section 2)

Racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious and similar beliefs, membership of a political
body, trade union membership, genetic and biometric data, sexual orientation and sexual life — and,
beyond the list, the language a person speaks (it reveals Maya, Garifuna or Mennonite origin) and
gender identity.

Categorical columns keep their shape: a yes/no flag stays a flag in the same vocabulary, a numeric
code gets other digits, and any other value is replaced from a list of the category (the ethnic
groups, languages and religions of the 2022 census, the parties of the 2025 general election).
Where the values share no vocabulary — beliefs, trade union, sexual life — they are suppressed to
`Not stated`. Biometric and genetic data become `X`.

### HEALTH — Health records

Not on the section 2 list, but health records have a place of their own in the Act (s. 2, 3, 10).
Health identifiers (BHIS, NHI, medical record), ICD-10 codes and diagnoses, medications, blood group
and disability are replaced within their vocabulary; clinical notes are redacted; reproductive and
mental health are suppressed.

### FIN — Financial record or position (s. 2 (i))

Sensitive in Belize. Credit references (scores keep their digits' shape, statuses are replaced),
debt, income, assets and pensions (mapped into plausible ranges in Belize dollars), Social Security
Board benefits, BOOST and other social programmes, poverty status and quintiles, and housing tenure.

### PENAL — Criminal record and proceedings (s. 2 (j), (k))

Records and proceedings replaced by a generic status whatever their outcome, flags kept as flags;
case numbers masked character by character.

## Towns and villages

The set carries the population of the 221 cities, towns, villages and communities of the 2022 census
and their location. 208 have fewer than 5,000 inhabitants and become the nearest of the 13 places of
their district that have at least 5,000 (Toledo has two, Corozal and Stann Creek one each). The
district itself is kept: the smallest has 38,944 people. A name shared by several places is
generalized only when all of them are small, and common spellings (`Corozal`, `Benque Viejo`,
`San Pedro`, `Mango Creek`) are recognized.

## Limits

- **Binary columns** (scans of the social security card or passport, fingerprints, clinical PDFs in
  BLOBs) cannot be masked by these algorithms. Inventory them and report them as residual risk.
- **Electoral divisions are not masked.** Generalize them to the district where they sit next to a
  birth date and sex.
- **No check digit is recomputed**: the social security number has no public one and the TIN's is not
  published. Company TINs are masked too.
- **Passport, permit and licence numbers** have no public format and are masked character by character.
- **Data Cleansing leaves unknown values as they are**: a village spelled in a way that is not in the
  list passes unchanged; villages sharing a name across districts follow the largest of them.
- **Free text** is redacted token by token: a phone written with spaces is not found there.
- **Children** are persons under 16 under the Act: a birth date moves at most 60 days, so few people
  cross their 16th or 18th birthday — but some do. Split the rule by age group with Multi-Column
  Condition where that matters.
- **The engine key** links masked values back to production for whoever holds both; keep it away from
  the users of the masked copy.

## Checking

```bash
node presets/belize-dpa-2021/build.mjs
node presets/belize-dpa-2021/verify.mjs
node presets/_doc/build.mjs belize-dpa-2021
```

The last one regenerates the PDFs; run it after any change to the set or to `doc/`.

`verify.mjs` reviews every classifier configuration, profiles 141 columns with the whole set at its
threshold — well named, badly named and 32 columns that are not personal data — and audits the
generalization table for all 221 places. It then masks 145 sample values, 198 placeholders and each
of the 90 algorithms' own sample input through the running app (`DLPX_URL`, default
`http://localhost:3000`), checking outputs against their vocabulary, and 150 social security numbers
for collisions. Each value forks a JVM.
