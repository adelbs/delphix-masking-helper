# Argentina — Ley 25.326

A profile set that finds Argentine personal data in a database and masks it, built for
[Law 25.326](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/60000-64999/64790/texact.htm) on the
Protection of Personal Data, implemented by Decree 1558/2001 and enforced by the Agencia de Acceso a la
Información Pública (AAIP). Personal data is information about identified or identifiable natural **or legal**
persons (art. 2); dissociated data — data that can no longer be associated with anyone — is not. The AAIP's
recommended security measures (Resolution 47/2018) name masking or dissociation for development, test and QA
environments. The goal is a non-production copy that no longer identifies anyone: not only the direct
identifiers, but the quasi-identifiers that re-identify people in combination, the sensitive data of art. 2 —
racial and ethnic origin, political opinions, religious, philosophical or moral convictions, trade union
membership, health and sexual life —, the genetic and biometric data of Convention 108+, gender identity, victims
of gender violence, credit information (art. 26) and criminal records (art. 7.4).

## Detection

Every domain has a **Path** classifier on the column name, in Spanish, in the abbreviations of Argentine
systems (`NRO_DOC`, `APYNOM`, `FEC_NAC`, `COD_LOCALIDAD`, `OBRA_SOCIAL`, `SITUACION_BCRA`) and in English.
Names that are ambiguous on their own (`NOMBRE`, `DOCUMENTO`, `PARTIDO` — political party or the partido of an
address —, `DEPARTAMENTO` — the flat or the geographic unit) carry a lower strength and need the values to agree;
generic ones exclude what they are not (`NOMBRE_PRODUCTO`, `ID_MENSAJE`).

Where the values have a recognizable shape or vocabulary, a **Regex** or **List** classifier finds the data in
columns whose name says nothing (`CAMPO1`, `OBS`): DNIs with dots, CUITs, CBUs, company names ending in S.A. or
S.R.L., given names and surnames, e-mail, Argentine phones, addresses with floor and flat or block and lot, cards
(Luhn), IPs, MAC and IMEI, Mercosur and older plates, localidades, partidos, CPA postal codes, indigenous peoples
and languages, religions, parties, unions, obras sociales, ICD-10 codes, medicines, blood groups, BCRA debtor
situations and social benefits.

**Type** classifiers rule domains out where the column cannot hold the data: an e-mail is text of at least 5
characters, a birth date a date, timestamp or text, an amount a number.

## Masking

All algorithms are deterministic for the engine's key, so the same value masks the same way in every table and
every job. Placeholders such as `N/A` or `-` do not fail a row: where there is nothing to mask they pass through.

### DNI, CUIT, CUIL, CBU and CVU

The DNI has no check digit; it is masked digit by digit, dots kept, one-to-one. A document column that mixes
DNIs, CUITs and passports is masked value by value by shape.

CUIT and CUIL keep a **valid check digit** (weights 5 4 3 2 7 6 5 4 3 2, modulus 11). When the remainder is 1 no
digit exists and ARCA changes the type instead — 20 and 27 become 23, 30 becomes 33, 23 becomes 24, 33 becomes 34.
Check Digit writes 11 − r and gets that case wrong, so each CUIT goes through two steps: a Check Digit with
doubled weights keeps the type, masks the eight digits and writes an auxiliary digit that is 9 exactly when the
true remainder is 1; a Regex Decompose then rewrites type and digit, or recomputes the digit with the true
weights. The eight digits are masked like the DNI, so a person's CUIL still holds their masked DNI.

CBU and CVU keep the bank block and both **valid check digits**: the 13-digit account is masked and its modulus-10
digit recomputed by a single Check Digit.

### L3 — Sensitive data (art. 2 and 7)

Ethnic origin (the 2022 census questions), indigenous peoples and languages, religion, philosophical or moral
convictions, political opinions, party membership, trade union membership (including union dues on payslips),
biometric and genetic data (Convention 108+), sexual orientation and sexual life, **gender identity** (Law 26.743,
whose registry correction is confidential) and **victims** of gender violence, trafficking and sexual crimes.

Categorical columns keep their shape: a yes/no flag stays a flag in the same vocabulary, a numeric code is replaced
by another code, and any other value is replaced from a list of the category. Where the values share no vocabulary
— convictions, unions, sexual life, victims — they are suppressed to `Sin información`. Biometric and genetic data
become `X`.

### SALUD — Health data

Health identifiers, obra social and prepaid plan (the RNOS code tells the union), ICD-10 codes with or without the
dot, procedures, medicines, clinical notes, test results, **HIV, hepatitis, tuberculosis and STIs** (Law 27.675),
blood group, disability and CUD, occupational health (ART), sexual and reproductive health (Law 27.610) and mental
health (Law 26.657).

### FIN — Financial and socioeconomic data (art. 26)

The **BCRA debtor situation** (replaced within its band: 1–2, 3–4, 5–6) and bureau scores, debt, income, assets and
pensions in pesos, ANSES benefits and social programmes, the **monotributo category** (replaced within its tier:
A–D, E–H, I–K) and housing tenure.

### PENAL — Criminal records and court files (art. 7.4)

Criminal records, misdemeanours and procedural situation replaced by a generic status, flags kept; court file
numbers (`CCC 12345/2023`, `PP-07-00-012345-23/00`) keep court, office and year and have the number masked.

## Geography

The set carries the 2022 census population of the 528 partidos, departamentos and comunas and of the 2,280
gobiernos locales, the 4,027 INDEC localidades censales, and the Georef location of each. 1,952 gobiernos locales
have fewer than 20,000 inhabitants (7 million people, 15%) and 203 departamentos do (2 million); each becomes the
nearest one of its province with at least 20,000. A localidad follows its gobierno local; one outside any gobierno
local is generalized too. A name shared by several places — or differing only in accents — is generalized only
when all are small; written with the province (`San José - Entre Ríos`, `San José (Entre Ríos)`) the same rule
applies within the province.

## Limits

- **Binary columns** (DNI photos, biometric selfies, clinical PDFs in BLOBs) cannot be masked by these algorithms.
  Inventory them and report them as residual risk.
- **A small localidad inside a large gobierno local** stays: the census gives population by gobierno local, not by
  localidad.
- **In Buenos Aires province** the gobierno local is the partido, so a small town can become a partido name that is
  not its main town (`Carhué` → `Saavedra`, whose main town is Pigüé).
- **A seven-digit DNI** is masked with seven digits, but inside the CUIL it carries a leading zero and is masked
  with eight: that person's DNI and CUIL no longer agree.
- **A check digit in a column of its own** is masked, not recomputed.
- **Province, the CBU's bank and the phone area code are not masked.**
- **Data Cleansing leaves unknown values as they are**: a localidad spelled in a way that is not in the table passes
  unchanged.
- **Free text** is redacted token by token: a phone written with spaces is not found there.
- **Children and adolescents**: a birth date moves at most 60 days, so few people cross their 13th, 16th or 18th
  birthday — but some do. Split the rule by age group with Multi-Column Condition where that matters.
- **The engine key** links masked values back to production for whoever holds both — the copy is then not
  dissociated in the sense of art. 2 and is still personal data; keep the key away from the users of the masked copy.

## Checking

```bash
node presets/argentina-ley-25326/build.mjs
node presets/argentina-ley-25326/verify.mjs
node presets/_doc/build.mjs argentina-ley-25326
```

The last one regenerates the PDFs; run it after any change to the set or to `doc/`.

`verify.mjs` reviews every classifier configuration, profiles 160 columns with the whole set at its threshold and
again with the essential pack — well named, badly named and 31 columns that are not personal data — and audits the
generalization tables for all 528 departamentos and all 2,280 gobiernos locales and 4,027 localidades. It then
masks the sample values, placeholders and each algorithm's own sample input through the running app (`DLPX_URL`,
default `http://localhost:3000`), checking outputs against their vocabulary; 240 CUITs of every type for validity
and collisions, type changes included; 150 CBUs for validity and collisions; 150 DNIs for collisions; and 20 people
whose DNI and CUIL must still agree. Each value forks a JVM.
