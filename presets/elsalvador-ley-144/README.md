# El Salvador — Ley para la Protección de Datos Personales

A profile set that finds Salvadoran personal data in a database and masks it, built for the
[Ley para la Protección de Datos Personales](https://www.asamblea.gob.sv/sites/default/files/documents/decretos/7A4FBD85-7E1B-46BE-9408-6FC549E53E00.pdf)
(Legislative Decree 144 of 12 November 2024, in force since 24 November 2024), El Salvador's first general
data protection law, applied and supervised by the Agencia de Ciberseguridad del Estado (ACE). Personal data
is information about an identified or identifiable natural person (art. 4 f); **dissociation or
anonymization** is irreversible (art. 4 h) and **pseudonymization** keeps the additional information
separate, hidden, classified and safeguarded (art. 4 q). The goal is a non-production copy that no longer
identifies anyone: not only the direct identifiers, but the quasi-identifiers that re-identify people in
combination, the sensitive data of art. 4 g) — ethnic origin, religious convictions, political affiliation,
trade unions, sexual preferences, physical and mental health, biometric and genetic data, **moral and family
situation** and **personal habits** —, the nationality of art. 59 b), financial data and criminal records.

## Detection

Every domain has a **Path** classifier on the column name, in Spanish, in the abbreviations of Salvadoran
systems (`NUM_DOC`, `FEC_NAC`, `COD_MUNICIPIO`, `NRC`, `ISSS`, `CENTRO_ESCOLAR`) and in English. Names that
are ambiguous on their own (`NOMBRE`, `DOCUMENTO`, `EXPEDIENTE`, `CARGO`) carry a lower strength and need the
values to agree; generic ones exclude what they are not (`NOMBRE_PRODUCTO`, `ID_MENSAJE`, `NIVEL_EDUCATIVO`).

Where the values have a recognizable shape or vocabulary, a **Regex** or **List** classifier finds the data in
columns whose name says nothing (`CAMPO1`, `OBS`): DUIs, fourteen-digit NITs, NRCs, eight-digit phones,
addresses with colonia and polígono, cards (Luhn), bitcoin addresses, IPs, MAC and IMEI, plates, distritos,
ethnic groups, indigenous languages, religions, parties, ICD-10 codes, medicines, blood groups and coverage.

**Type** classifiers rule domains out where the column cannot hold the data: an e-mail is text of at least 5
characters, a birth date a date, timestamp or text, an amount a number.

## Masking

All algorithms are deterministic for the engine's key, so the same value masks the same way in every table and
every job. Placeholders such as `N/A` or `-` do not fail a row: where there is nothing to mask they pass through.

### DUI and NIT

The **DUI** keeps a valid check digit: weights 9 8 7 6 5 4 3 2 from the left, modulus 10, ten minus the
remainder — exactly what Check Digit computes, so one step masks the eight digits and writes the ninth. Since
Legislative Decree 203 of 2021 the DUI number is also the NIT of an adult natural person.

The fourteen-digit **NIT** — municipality, **date of birth**, correlative and check digit — has two rules,
chosen by the correlative. Above 100 it is eleven minus the remainder (modulus 11, weights 2 7 6 5 4 3 2 7 6 5
4 3 2), which Check Digit writes directly. At 100 or below the digit **is** the remainder, which Check Digit
cannot write — so the set uses the **complementary weights** (eleven minus each weight, modulo 11), inverting
the remainder so that what Check Digit writes is the true one. Each branch keeps the correlative in its class:
the old one leaves a leading zero, the new one maps it into 101–999 with Numeric Mapping. The whole body is
masked, so the birth date inside a NIT does not survive.

### L1 — Direct identifiers

| Domain | Masking |

| `SV_L1_NOMBRE`, `SV_L1_APELLIDO` | Word by word from Salvadoran lists; `de`, `del`, `la` stay |

### L3 — Sensitive data (art. 4 g and art. 59 b)

Ethnic origin (2024 census categories), indigenous language, **nationality** (named in art. 59 b beside ethnic
origin), religion, spiritual or philosophical convictions, political ideology and party membership, trade union
membership, biometric and genetic data, sexual preferences and sexual life, gender identity — and two
categories the law spells out and no other in the region does: the **moral and family situation** and the
**personal habits** that cookies profile.

Categorical columns keep their shape: a yes/no flag stays a flag in the same vocabulary, a numeric code is
replaced by another code, and any other value is replaced from a list of the category. Where the values share no
vocabulary — convictions, unions, sexual life, family situation, habits, victims — they are suppressed to
`Sin información`. Biometric and genetic data become `X`.

### SALUD — Health data (art. 4 g and art. 39)

Clinical record numbers, coverage (ISSS, MINSAL, Bienestar Magisterial, private), ICD-10 codes with or without
the dot, procedures, medicines, clinical notes, test results, **HIV and STIs** in a domain of their own, blood
group, disability, occupational, reproductive and mental health.

### FIN — Financial and socioeconomic data

Credit history (art. 3 a leaves it to its own law), debt, income, assets and pensions in dollars,
**remittances**, subsidies and social programmes, and housing tenure.

### PENAL — Criminal records

Record certificates and procedural situation replaced by a generic status, flags kept; court and prosecution
file numbers keep the unit and the year and have the correlative masked.

## Geography

The set carries the population of the 2024 census and the location of the 262 distritos and the 44 municipios,
as the Banco Central de Reserva publishes them. The 2023 restructuring turned the former 262 municipalities
into distritos: 190 of them have fewer than 20,000 inhabitants (1.5 million people, a quarter of the country)
and become the nearest distrito of their municipio with at least 20,000 — of their departamento when the
municipio has none. The 44 municipios and the 14 departamentos stay. A name shared by several distritos — El
Rosario, San José, San Isidro — is generalized only when all are small; written with the departamento
(`El Rosario - La Paz`) the same rule applies inside it.

## Limits

- **Binary columns** (DUI photos, fingerprints, clinical PDFs in BLOBs) cannot be masked by these algorithms.
  Inventory them and report them as residual risk.
- **The NRC check digit is not recomputed**: its rule is not public.
- **A check digit in a column of its own** is masked, not recomputed.
- **Departamento, municipio, bank code and phone prefix are not masked.**
- **Data Cleansing leaves unknown values as they are**: a distrito spelled in a way that is not in the table
  passes unchanged.
- **Free text** is redacted token by token: a phone written with spaces is not found there.
- **Masked bitcoin addresses** no longer pass their own checksum — deliberately.
- **Children and adolescents**: a birth date moves at most 60 days, so few people cross their 12th or 18th
  birthday — but some do. Split the rule by age group with Multi-Column Condition where that matters.
- **The engine key** links masked values back to production for whoever holds both — the copy is pseudonymized
  (art. 4 q), not dissociated, and still personal data; reversing pseudonymization without consent is a very
  serious infringement (art. 56 c, 8).

## Checking

```bash
node presets/elsalvador-ley-144/build.mjs
node presets/elsalvador-ley-144/verify.mjs
node presets/_doc/build.mjs elsalvador-ley-144
```

The last one regenerates the PDFs; run it after any change to the set or to `doc/`.

`verify.mjs` reviews every classifier configuration, profiles 146 columns with the whole set at its threshold
and again with the essential pack — well named, badly named and 30 columns that are not personal data — and
audits the generalization table for all 262 distritos. It then masks the sample values, placeholders and each
algorithm's own sample input through the running app (`DLPX_URL`, default `http://localhost:3000`), checking
outputs against their vocabulary; 150 DUIs and 200 NITs of both rules for validity and collisions, and 20 NITs
to confirm the birth date inside them does not survive. Each value forks a JVM.
