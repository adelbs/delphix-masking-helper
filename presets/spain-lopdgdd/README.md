# Spain — GDPR and Ley Orgánica 3/2018 (LOPDGDD)

A profile set that finds Spanish personal data in a database and masks it, built for the
[General Data Protection Regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) — which
applies directly in Spain — and the
[Ley Orgánica 3/2018, de 5 de diciembre, de Protección de Datos Personales y garantía de los
derechos digitales](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2018-16673) (LOPDGDD), as last
amended by Ley 10/2025 of 26 December. The authority is the **Agencia Española de Protección de
Datos (AEPD)**.

Three things make this set different from the others.

- **Spanish law reaches further than the Regulation in three places this set leans on.** Article
  9.1 of the LOPDGDD says consent alone does not lift the prohibition of processing data whose main
  purpose is to identify ideology, trade union membership, religion, sexual orientation, beliefs or
  racial or ethnic origin. Article 32 orders rectified and erased data to be **blocked** — reserved
  "para impedir su tratamiento, incluyendo su visualización" — and failing to do so is a very
  serious infringement. And article 72.1 p) makes **"la reversión deliberada de un procedimiento de
  anonimización"** a very serious infringement too.
- **Non-production copies have a rule of their own.** The AEPD asked in 2022 that development and
  pre-production environments avoid personal data, and the **Esquema Nacional de Seguridad** (Real
  Decreto 311/2022) requires, in measure mp.sw.1, "ni datos de producción en el de desarrollo" for
  systems of medium and high category — the whole public sector and its suppliers.
- **Almost every Spanish identifier carries check characters, and this set recomputes all of
  them**: the letter of the DNI and the NIE (modulo 23), the control digits of the Seguridad Social
  number (modulo 97), the second control digit of the bank account and the IBAN digits (modulo 11
  and 97), the control letters of the referencia catastral (weighted modulo 23) and of the CUPS
  (modulo 529). No framework writes any of them; the set computes them with lookup tables.

## Detection

Every domain has a **Path** classifier on the column name — in Castilian, in English and, for the
data most systems hold, in **Catalan, Galician and Basque** (`NOM`, `COGNOMS`, `ADREÇA`, `ENDEREZO`,
`IZENA`, `ABIZENAK`, `DATA_NAIXEMENT`) — and in the abbreviations of Spanish systems (`NAF`, `NUSS`,
`NHC`, `CIP`, `NIA`, `CUPS`, `NIG`, `IRPF`). Names that are ambiguous on their own (`NOMBRE`,
`DOCUMENTO`, `HISTORIA`, `PROCEDIMIENTO`) carry a lower strength and need the values to agree;
generic ones exclude what they are not (`NOMBRE_PRODUCTO`, `TIPO_DOCUMENTO`, and `NIF_EMPRESA`,
which must not read as a person's NIF).

Where the values have a recognizable shape or vocabulary, a **Regex** or **List** classifier finds
the data in columns whose name says nothing (`CAMPO1`, `OBS`): DNIs and NIEs whose letter is one of
the 23 of the table, Seguridad Social numbers, IBANs and CCCs, referencias catastrales, CUPS, plates
of the current series, phones by their first digit, street addresses by their type of street,
cards (Luhn), IPs, MAC and IMEI, municipio names, ethnic and religious vocabularies, Spanish parties
and unions, CIE-10-ES codes, medicines, blood groups, coverage by MUFACE, ISFAS and MUGEJU,
dependency grades, benefits such as the Ingreso Mínimo Vital, and NIGs.

**Type** classifiers rule domains out where the column cannot hold the data: an e-mail is text of at
least 5 characters, a birth date a date, timestamp or text, an amount a number.

## Masking

All algorithms are deterministic for the engine's key, so the same value masks the same way in every
table and every job. Placeholders such as `N/A` or `-` do not fail a row: where there is nothing to
mask they pass through.

### Check characters computed by lookup tables

A **state** — the remainder so far, written with as many digits as the modulus needs — is put in
front of the value and travels through it one character at a time. Each step is a Regex Decompose
that hands "state + next character" to a Data Cleansing whose table answers "next character + new
state"; separators and characters that do not count are stepped over. At the end a last table turns
the remainder into the check characters. A step that finds no line for its window leaves the value
as it is, so one chain serves every written form.

| Identifier | Rule | Table | Steps |
|---|---|---|---|
| DNI, NIE, NIF K/L/M | number modulo 23, letter of `TRWAGMYFPDXBNJZSQVHLCKE` | 299 + 46 lines | 12 |
| Seguridad Social | first ten digits modulo 97 (a leading zero of the number does not count) | 1,358 + 97 | 14 |
| CUPS | sixteen digits modulo 529, two letters of the DNI table | 6,877 + 529 | 22 |
| Referencia catastral | eleven characters × 13 15 12 5 4 17 9 21 3 7 1 modulo 23, letter of `MQWERTYUIOPASDFGHJKLBZX` | 11 × 230 + 828 + 529 | 18 (urban), 12 (rural) |
| CCC (second digit) | account × 1 2 4 8 5 10 9 7 3 6 modulo 11, 10 → 1, 11 → 0 | 121 + 110 | 11, right to left |

The **bank account** combines the plugin's IBAN algorithm with tables. The IBAN algorithm masks the
ten digits of the account and recomputes the two ISO 13616 digits, but it does not know the second
control digit of the Spanish account, which sits between the branch and the account. The set
recomputes that digit with a state crossing the account from right to left, carries the old and the
new digit to the front as a pair (a table of 1,100 lines), and corrects the IBAN digits for the
change with a table of 10,000 lines — the second digit weighs 10¹⁶ in the number the IBAN checks.
Bank, branch and first control digit stay. Compact (`ES9121000418450200051332`) and printed in
groups of four (`ES91 2100 0418 4502 0005 1332`) come out valid under both rules.

The referencia catastral algorithm was checked against references published in the BOE
(`8943507PB5284S0001KR`, `5991807UL6359S0001KP`, `3697301BC4839N0001KF`) and the Catastro's own
example (`9872023VH5797S0001WX`); the CUPS one against the python-stdnum example
(`ES1234123456789012JY`).

| Domain | Masking |

### L3 — Special categories (art. 9 RGPD and art. 9 LOPDGDD)

Racial or ethnic origin, religion, philosophical beliefs and conscientious objection, political
opinion on the CIS scale and party membership, trade union membership, biometric and genetic data,
sexual orientation and sex life — and **gender identity** (Ley 4/2023, Ley 15/2022) and the **victims
of gender and sexual violence** (LO 1/2004, LO 10/2022), which Spanish law protects in their own
right. Categorical columns keep their shape: a yes/no flag stays a flag, a numeric code becomes
another code, and any other value is replaced from a list of the category. Where the values share no
vocabulary — philosophical beliefs, unions, sex life, victims — they are suppressed to
`Sin información`. Biometric and genetic data become `X`.

### SALUD — Health data (art. 9 RGPD, Ley 41/2002, DA 17 LOPDGDD)

Historia clínica, CIP-SNS and the regional health-card codes, coverage (Seguridad Social, MUFACE,
ISFAS, MUGEJU, mutual and private insurers), **CIE-10-ES** diagnoses and procedures, medicines,
clinical text, test results, **HIV** and STIs, blood group, **disability** (RD 888/2022),
**dependency** (Ley 39/2006), occupational health and sick leave (Ley 31/1995, art. 22.4),
reproductive health (LO 2/2010), mental health.

### FIN — Economic and tax data (art. 20 LOPDGDD, art. 95 LGT)

Files of defaulters and the CIRBE, debt, income, assets, pension, social benefits (Ingreso Mínimo
Vital, unemployment, regional minimum income, social bonus) and **tax data** — which article 95 of
the Ley General Tributaria calls reserved; a refund keeps its sign. Amounts are replaced by another
amount of the same order of magnitude in euros.

### PENAL — Criminal and administrative offences (art. 10 RGPD, arts. 10 and 27 LOPDGDD)

Criminal records, the **certificate of the Registro Central de Delincuentes Sexuales** that article
57 of the LOPIVI requires of anyone working with minors, the **NIG** and the case number (the
correlative changes; court seat, jurisdiction and year stay), and **administrative sanctions** —
fines and the points of a driving licence —, which article 27 of the LOPDGDD reserves to the
authority that imposes them.

## Geography

The set carries the 8,132 municipios of the INE's Relación de municipios of 1 January 2026, with the
population of the Padrón of 1 January 2025. **7,700 of them have fewer than 20,000 inhabitants** —
14.5 million people, almost a third of the country — and become the nearest municipio of at least
20,000 in the same province, measured between centres from GeoNames (by INE code; the eight
municipios created after 2018 are placed by hand). Every province has a target — its capital at
least — so a generalized value never leaves its province. The threshold is the INE's own: it recoded
the municipio of everyone living in one of under 20,000 when it published the 2011 census
microdata.

Names come in many spellings, and the table carries them all: the INE's article behind
(`Guijarrosa, La`), both names of bilingual municipios (`Alacant/Alicante`, `Donostia/San Sebastián`),
the Castilian exonyms of large cities (`La Coruña`, `Gerona`, `Lérida`, `Orense`), capitals and no
accents, and the forms with the province beside them (`Villanueva (Toledo)`, `Villanueva, Toledo`) —
68,972 lines. A name is generalized only when every place that bears it is small.

The **INE code** follows the name, with or without its check digit (15,400 lines). The **postal
code** follows the municipio with most street segments in the Callejero del Censo Electoral: a code
whose main municipio is small becomes the main code of the municipio it is generalized to — 8,638 of
10,790. The Callejero files a few segments under a municipio of another province; eight codes have
no municipio of their own province at all (one of them with a province 56 that does not exist), and
the build leaves those out.

## Limits

- **Masking with a key is pseudonymization.** The masked copy is still personal data for whoever
  holds the key and production; keep them apart — the separation the additional provision 17 of the
  LOPDGDD asks for health research.
- **Blocked data (art. 32 LOPDGDD) must not be copied at all.** Masking does not make copying a
  blocked row lawful; leave blocked rows out of the subset the job reads.
- **Populations are those of 1 January 2025.** Rebuild when the INE publishes the next revision.
- **A postal code may serve several municipios**; the set follows the main one, so the code of a
  large town that also serves small villages is kept.
- **Data Cleansing leaves unknown values as they are**: an old name, a typo, or a hamlet
  (entidad singular de población) instead of its municipio passes unchanged.
- **Bank and branch are kept** in an account; the branch can tell a town.
- **A referencia catastral gets its letters recomputed only in its compact twenty-character form.**
- **A single `M` is read as mujer.** A column coded F/M, like the DNI's machine-readable zone, needs
  its own algorithm.
- **Free text** is redacted word by word; a phone written `612 34 56 78` is reached in pieces.
- **Children**: a 60-day shift moves a few people across the 14th birthday, the age of digital
  consent in Spain (art. 7 LOPDGDD), or the 18th. Split by age with Multi-Column Condition where it
  matters.
- **Binary columns** (scanned documents, photographs, PDFs) cannot be masked by these algorithms.
  Inventory them and report them as residual risk.

## Checking

```bash
node presets/spain-lopdgdd/build.mjs
node presets/spain-lopdgdd/verify.mjs
node presets/_doc/build.mjs spain-lopdgdd
```

The last one regenerates the PDFs; run it after any change to the set or to `doc/`.

The build itself runs each check-character chain with the masking taken as the identity and fails
unless a valid example comes back unchanged. `verify.mjs` reviews every classifier configuration,
profiles 159 columns with the whole set at its threshold and again with the essential pack — well
named, badly named, named in Catalan, Galician or Basque, and 31 columns that are not personal data,
among them the NIF of a company — and audits the generalization for all 8,132 municipios, 10,790
postal codes and 52 provinces, checking that every one of the 68,972 replacements names a municipio
of at least 20,000. It then masks the sample values, placeholders and each algorithm's own sample
input through the running app (`DLPX_URL`, default `http://localhost:3000`), and masks in bulk 250
DNIs, 100 NIEs, 200 Seguridad Social numbers, 200 IBANs (compact and printed), 100 CCCs, 150
referencias catastrales and 100 CUPS, checking that every output is **valid under its own rule**,
distinct, of the same shape, and keeps what it should keep. Each value forks a JVM; the whole run
takes about eight minutes.
