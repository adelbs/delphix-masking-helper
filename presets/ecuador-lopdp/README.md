# Ecuador — Ley Orgánica de Protección de Datos Personales

A profile set that finds Ecuadorian personal data in a database and masks it, built for the
[Ley Orgánica de Protección de Datos Personales](https://www.finanzaspopulares.gob.ec/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/ley_organica_de_proteccion_de_datos_personales.pdf)
(Fifth Supplement of Official Register 459, 26 May 2021) and its
[Reglamento General](https://www.cosede.gob.ec/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/REGLAMENTO-GENERAL-A-LA-LEY-ORG%C3%81NICA-DE-PROTECCION-DE-DATOS-PERSONALES_compressed-1.pdf)
(Official Register Supplement 435, 13 November 2023); the sanctions regime has applied since 26 May
2023. Personal data is the datum that identifies or makes identifiable a natural person, directly or
indirectly (art. 4); **anonymization** prevents identification *and re-identification* without
disproportionate effort and takes the data out of the law (arts. 4 and 2 c), while
**pseudonymization** keeps the additional information apart and under technical and organizational
measures (art. 4) — the European pair, word for word. The goal is a non-production copy that no
longer identifies anyone: not only the direct identifiers, but the quasi-identifiers that
re-identify people in combination, the sensitive data of art. 4 — the longest list in the region,
naming the **identidad cultural**, the **condición migratoria** and the **pasado judicial** as no
neighbouring law does —, health data, the *datos personales crediticios* of arts. 28 and 29, and
criminal proceedings.

## Detection

Every domain has a **Path** classifier on the column name, in Spanish, in the abbreviations of
Ecuadorian systems (`NUM_DOC`, `FEC_NAC`, `DPA`, `RUC`, `IESS`, `AMIE`) and in English. Names that
are ambiguous on their own (`NOMBRE`, `DOCUMENTO`, `EXPEDIENTE`, `CARGO`) carry a lower strength and
need the values to agree; generic ones exclude what they are not (`NOMBRE_PRODUCTO`, `ID_MENSAJE`,
`PARROQUIA_ECLESIASTICA`).

Where the values have a recognizable shape or vocabulary, a **Regex** or **List** classifier finds
the data in columns whose name says nothing (`CAMPO1`, `OBS`): cédulas, thirteen-digit RUCs, mobiles
starting 09, addresses with ciudadela, manzana and villa, cards (Luhn), IPs, MAC and IMEI, plates,
parroquias, ethnic categories, indigenous nationalities and languages, migratory status, religions,
political organizations, ICD-10 codes, medicines, blood groups and health cover.

**Type** classifiers rule domains out where the column cannot hold the data: an e-mail is text of at
least 5 characters, a birth date a date, timestamp or text, an amount a number.

## Masking

All algorithms are deterministic for the engine's key, so the same value masks the same way in every
table and every job. Placeholders such as `N/A` or `-` do not fail a row: where there is nothing to
mask they pass through.

### Cédula and RUC

The **cédula** is ten digits: the province of issue (01–24, or 30 for a citizen registered abroad),
a digit that says the holder is a natural person (0–5), six more, and a check digit computed with the
coefficients 2 1 2 1 2 1 2 1 2, products over nine reduced by nine, ten minus the remainder modulo
ten — which is exactly the **Luhn algorithm**. The engine's **Payment Card** framework keeps the Luhn
residue of its input, so a valid cédula masks to another valid one in a single step; `preserve: 3`
keeps the province of issue and the natural-person digit. The province stays for the same reason the
provincia column stays: the set generalizes below the province, not at it.

The **RUC** is thirteen digits and its third one decides the rule. **0–5** is a natural person: the
RUC is the cédula plus the establishment code `001`, masked as the cédula it carries, so both still
agree. **9** is a private company (check digit in the tenth position, modulo 11, coefficients
4 3 2 7 6 5 4 3 2) and **6** a public body (check digit in the ninth, coefficients 3 2 7 6 5 4 3 2).
Neither is a natural person, and art. 4 makes only a natural person a data subject — so both are
left exactly as they are.

### L3 — Sensitive data (art. 4)

Etnia (2022 census categories), **identidad cultural** — the indigenous nationality or pueblo, which
no neighbouring law lists —, ancestral language, **condición migratoria** together with the stateless
and refugee status the same article names, nationality, religion, ideología, filiación política,
trade union membership, biometric and genetic data, sexual orientation, identidad de género and
victims of violence.

Categorical columns keep their shape: a yes/no flag stays a flag in the same vocabulary, a numeric
code is replaced by another code, and any other value is replaced from a list of the category. Where
the values share no vocabulary — unions, victims — they are suppressed to `Sin información`.
Biometric and genetic data become `X`.

### SALUD — Health data (art. 4, art. 25 c and arts. 30 to 32)

Art. 31.2 asks that health data be *previously anonymized or pseudonymized whenever possible*, which
is what this group is for: clinical record numbers, cover (IESS, Seguro Social Campesino, ISSFA,
ISSPOL, MSP, private), ICD-10 codes with or without the dot, procedures, medicines, clinical notes,
test results, **HIV and STIs** in a domain of their own, blood group, **disability** — a special
category of its own under art. 25 d), which also covers the *sustituto* —, occupational, reproductive
and mental health.

### FIN — Credit and socioeconomic data

The *datos personales crediticios* of arts. 28 and 29, with the risk categories A-1 to E of the
Superintendencia de Bancos; debt, income, assets and pensions in dollars; social programmes, the
Registro Social quintile and housing tenure.

### PENAL — Judicial record

**`pasado judicial` is sensitive data in Ecuador** (art. 4). Record certificates and procedural
situation replaced by a generic status, flags kept; court file numbers keep the year and have the
judicial unit and the correlative masked.

## Geography

The set carries the population the 2022 census counted and the location the DPA layer publishes for
the 1,040 parroquias and the 224 cantones. **913 of the parroquias have fewer than 20,000
inhabitants** (4.3 million people) and become the nearest parroquia of their cantón with at least
20,000 — of their provincia when the cantón has none. The 93 cantones under 20,000 follow the same
rule inside their provincia, and the 24 provincias stay. Three provincias have no parroquia of 20,000
at all — Galápagos, Zamora Chinchipe and the zona no delimitada: there the value stays inside the
provincia and becomes its most populous parroquia, a smaller group than elsewhere, rather than being
sent to another province. A name shared by several places — San José, Santa Rosa, Bellavista — is
generalized only when all of them are small, and then follows the most populous; when that bearer is
a cantón, the replacement is a cantón. Written with the cantón or the provincia
(`Facundo Vela - Bolívar`) the same rule applies inside it.

## Limits

- **Binary columns** (cédula photos, fingerprints, clinical PDFs in BLOBs) cannot be masked by these
  algorithms. Inventory them and report them as residual risk.
- **The province of issue of the cédula stays**, and so does the provincia. Changing those two digits
  breaks the Luhn digit, which would then have to be recomputed.
- **A company RUC is not masked**: art. 4 makes only a natural person a data subject.
- **The DPA layer publishes place names without their acute accents.** The set restores them with a
  dictionary and writes both spellings into the tables, but a name it could not restore comes out
  unaccented.
- **Data Cleansing leaves unknown values as they are**: a parroquia spelled in a way that is not in
  the table passes unchanged.
- **Free text** is redacted token by token: a phone written with spaces is not always found.
- **Masked bitcoin addresses** no longer pass their own checksum — deliberately.
- **Children and adolescents** are a special category of their own (art. 25 b): a birth date moves at
  most 60 days, so few people cross their 18th birthday — but some do. Split the rule by age group
  with Multi-Column Condition where that matters.
- **The engine key** links masked values back to production for whoever holds both — the copy is
  *pseudonymized* in the sense of art. 4, not anonymized, and still personal data. Keep the key apart
  and under technical and organizational measures, which is what art. 4 asks and art. 37 counts as a
  security measure.

## Checking

```bash
node presets/ecuador-lopdp/build.mjs
node presets/ecuador-lopdp/verify.mjs
node presets/_doc/build.mjs ecuador-lopdp
```

The last one regenerates the PDFs; run it after any change to the set or to `doc/`.

`verify.mjs` reviews every classifier configuration, profiles 142 columns with the whole set at its
threshold and again with the essential pack — well named, badly named and 29 columns that are not
personal data — and audits the generalization table for all 1,040 parroquias and 224 cantones. It
then masks the sample values, placeholders and each algorithm's own sample input through the running
app (`DLPX_URL`, default `http://localhost:3000`), checking outputs against their vocabulary; 200
cédulas for validity, distinctness and the province and type they keep, 25 cédula and RUC pairs to
confirm they still agree, and 40 company RUCs to confirm they come back untouched. Each value forks
a JVM.
