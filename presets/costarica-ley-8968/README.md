# Costa Rica — Ley 8968 de Protección de la Persona frente al Tratamiento de sus Datos Personales

A profile set that finds Costa Rican personal data in a database and masks it, built for the
[Ley 8968 de Protección de la Persona frente al Tratamiento de sus Datos Personales](https://www.pgrweb.go.cr/scij/Busqueda/Normativa/Normas/nrm_texto_completo.aspx?nValor1=1&nValor2=70975)
(7 July 2011, in force since 5 September 2011) and its
[Reglamento, Decreto Ejecutivo 37554-JP](https://www.tse.go.cr/pdf/normativa/reglamentoleyproteccionpersona.pdf),
as amended by Decreto 40008-JP (2016) and Decreto 41582 (2019). The authority is the **Agencia de
Protección de Datos de los Habitantes (Prodhab)**, created by article 15. The law itself has never
been amended.

Three things make this set different from its neighbours.

- **The law names the technique.** Article 6.1 says that data which may affect its holder shall not
  be kept beyond ten years from the facts recorded, and that if it must be kept longer it shall be
  **"desasociados de su titular"**. Article 2 r) of the Reglamento defines the *procedimiento de
  desasociación*, and article 2 q) makes identifiability relative: a person "no se considerará
  identificable si dicha identificación requiere plazos o actividades desproporcionadas".
- **Sensitive data is prohibited, not regulated.** Article 9.1 forbids processing data revealing
  racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious, spiritual or philosophical convictions,
  health, sexual life and orientation, "entre otros"; article 31 a) makes doing so a **falta
  gravísima** for a private party — fifteen to thirty base salaries and the suspension of the
  fichero for up to six months. Article 3 e) adds a category no other law of the region lists:
  the **condición socioeconómica**.
- **No identity number carries a check digit.** The cédula, the DIMEX, the NITE and the cédula
  jurídica are validated against a register, not by arithmetic, so the whole number can change —
  the provincia digit included. The one place arithmetic matters is the **IBAN**, mandatory since
  2017, and this set recomputes its two check digits.

## Detection

Every domain has a **Path** classifier on the column name, in Spanish, in the abbreviations of
Costa Rican systems (`CED`, `DIMEX`, `NITE`, `FEC_NAC`, `FOLIO_REAL`, `EDUS`, `SUGEF`, `SINIRUBE`)
and in English. Names that are ambiguous on their own (`NOMBRE`, `DOCUMENTO`, `EXPEDIENTE`, `CARGO`)
carry a lower strength and need the values to agree; generic ones exclude what they are not
(`NOMBRE_PRODUCTO`, `ID_MENSAJE`, `TIPO_IDENTIFICACION`, and `CEDULA_JURIDICA`, which must not read
as `CEDULA`).

Where the values have a recognizable shape or vocabulary, a **Regex** or **List** classifier finds
the data in columns whose name says nothing (`CAMPO1`, `OBS`): cédulas written `1-0234-0567`,
eleven- and twelve-digit DIMEX, NITE of the 3-120 series, IBANs compact and printed in groups of
four, eight-digit phones by their first digit, addresses with metres and a direction from a
landmark, cards (Luhn), IPs, MAC and IMEI, plates with their class prefix, folios reales, distritos
and cantones, indigenous peoples, religions, parties, ICD-10 codes, medicines, blood groups, health
régimen, SUGEF categories and judicial file numbers.

**Type** classifiers rule domains out where the column cannot hold the data: an e-mail is text of at
least 5 characters, a birth date a date, timestamp or text, an amount a number.

## Masking

All algorithms are deterministic for the engine's key, so the same value masks the same way in every
table and every job. Placeholders such as `N/A` or `-` do not fail a row: where there is nothing to
mask they pass through.

### The IBAN and its check digits

Since 2017 every Costa Rican account is an IBAN of 22 characters: `CR`, two check digits, a reserved
zero, three digits for the bank and fourteen for the account — the 17-digit Cuenta Cliente of SINPE
with five characters in front. The check digits are the ISO 13616 ones: move `CRkk` to the end, read
`C` as 12 and `R` as 27, and the number must leave a remainder of **1 modulo 97**. Masking the
account without rewriting them produces an IBAN every library rejects.

No framework computes a remainder modulo 97, and Check Digit writes one character where these are
two, so the set computes it with lookup tables, the way the Panama set computes the DV of a RUC. A
**two-digit state** — the remainder so far — replaces the old check digits and travels right through
the number one character at a time; each step is a Data Cleansing that turns "state + character"
into "character + new state", from a table of 1,067 lines. At the right end a table of 97 lines
turns the remainder into the check digits, by 98 − (r·10⁶ + 1227·100) mod 97. A second traveller
carries the two digits back to the front, where `CR` and the spaces of the printed form wait as
one-character markers. The chain is 49 steps, nested in parts of eight.

The bank code stays: it is not personal data, and keeping it keeps a test account inside a real bank.
Both written forms come out as they went in — `CR05015202001026284066` and
`CR05 0152 0200 1026 2840 66` — valid, with their bank, and with an account that belongs to nobody.

### Identity documents

The **cédula de identidad** is ten digits in canonical form, `0P-TTTT-AAAA`, usually written
`1-0234-0567` or `102340567`, and it carries **no check digit**. So every digit changes, the
provincia digit of the birth entry included — it is masked inside 1 to 9, never to a zero, so the
result is still a well-formed cédula. A **DIMEX** of eleven or twelve digits changes entirely and
keeps its length, which is what distinguishes it from a cédula in a mixed column.

The **NITE** is where the law draws its sharpest line. The `3-120` series belongs to a foreign
natural person and is masked; the `3-130` series belongs to a foreign company and is left alone. A
**cédula jurídica**, `3-101-123456` — and from the fourth quarter of 2026 `3-101-A00001` under
Decreto 44648-MJ — passes through untouched: article 3 b) and g) reach only a natural person, so a
company is no titular in Costa Rica.

### L3 — Sensitive data (art. 3 e and art. 9.1)

Racial or ethnic origin, **indigenous people and territorio indígena** — eight peoples and 24
territories, a few thousand and a few hundred people each —, nationality and **condición
migratoria**, religion, **convicciones filosóficas** (named in art. 9.1, which art. 3 e) leaves
out), political opinion and party adherence, trade union membership, biometric and genetic data,
sexual orientation, **vida sexual** and gender identity, and victims of violence (Ley 8589, Ley
9095).

Categorical columns keep their shape: a yes/no flag stays a flag in the same vocabulary, a numeric
code is replaced by another code, and any other value is replaced from a list of the category. Where
the values share no vocabulary — philosophical convictions, unions, sexual life, victims — they are
suppressed to `Sin información`. Biometric and genetic data become `X`.

### SALUD — Health data (art. 9.1 d)

Expediente clínico and EDUS numbers, health régimen and coverage, ICD-10 diagnosis, procedure,
medicine, clinical notes, test results, **HIV** (Ley 7771), blood group, **disability** (Ley 7600,
Ley 9379), occupational health and the boleta de incapacidad, reproductive health, mental health.
Article 9.1 d) allows processing only for health care itself and only by someone bound to
professional secrecy — which a test environment is not.

### FIN — Economic data (art. 3 e, art. 1 and art. 9.4)

SUGEF category and the Centro de Información Crediticia, debt, income, patrimony, pension, social
programmes (Avancemos, Crecemos, BANHVI, FONABE, IMAS, RNC), **condición socioeconómica** (the
SINIRUBE classification and the ficha de información social) and housing tenure. Amounts are
replaced by another amount of the same order of magnitude in colones, so totals and ratios still
behave. Article 3 e) calls the condición socioeconómica sensitive and article 1 reaches the data of
a person's bienes; article 9.4 sends credit behaviour to the rules of the Sistema Financiero
Nacional without taking it out of the law.

### PENAL — Criminal records (art. 8 c and Ley 6723)

Hoja de delincuencia and juzgamientos, and the judicial file number `24-001234-0007-PE` — the
correlative changes, the year, the office and the matter stay. Article 11 of Ley 6723, as reformed
by **Ley 10453 of 4 March 2024**, cancels an entry on a scale that runs from the end of the sentence
to ten years after it; a non-production copy outlives every one of those terms.

## Geography

The set carries the Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Censos estimate of population for 2022 for
the 492 distritos, the 84 cantones and the 7 provincias, and the names, codes and barrios of the
**División Territorial Administrativa of 2025** (Decreto 44882-MGP). **413 of the 492 distritos have
fewer than 20,000 inhabitants** — some 2.68 million people, more than half the country — and become
the nearest distrito of at least 20,000 in the same cantón, or in the same provincia when the cantón
has none. The 84 cantones follow the same rule one level up, 15 of them being below the threshold,
and the 7 provincias stay, so a generalized value never leaves its provincia.

Costa Rica is full of homonyms: 179 distritos share a name with another place, and there are
distritos called San Rafael, San Isidro and San José in province after province. A name is
generalized only when **every** place that bears it is small, and then follows the most populous; 91
small distritos keep their own name because a homonym of theirs is large. Written with the cantón or
the provincia (`Matambú - Hojancha`, in five separator formats) the same rule applies inside the
qualifier.

The codes follow the names, and here Costa Rica gives something unique in the region: the DTA code —
one digit for the provincia, two for the cantón, two for the distrito — **is the postal code**, so
one table of 428 lines generalizes the distrito code, the cantón code and the postal code at once
and a masked postal code always agrees with the masked distrito beside it.

## Limits

- **Binary columns** (scanned cédulas, photographs, clinical PDFs in BLOBs) cannot be masked by
  these algorithms. Inventory them and report them as residual risk — the *riesgo residual* article
  36 f) of the Reglamento asks to be computed.
- **The populations are estimates.** They are the INEC estimate for 2022 projected from the 2011
  census, totalling some 5.20 million against the 5,044,197 the 2022 census counted — about 3%
  higher. A distrito within a few hundred of the threshold may fall on the other side of it under
  the census figures.
- **Sixteen distritos have no published figure of their own** — the three of Río Cuarto, Monteverde,
  Puerto Jiménez, Puente Salas, Quitirrisí, Matambú and others created after that distribution, plus
  the uninhabited Isla del Coco. The set treats them as small, which generalizes them: the
  conservative choice, at the cost of some utility.
- **La Palmera (210 09)** is written `LA PALMEIRA` in the 2025 decree; the set uses `La Palmera`, the
  spelling of the boundary set and of common use, and detects both.
- **Provincia, bank code and phone prefix are not masked.**
- **Data Cleansing leaves unknown values as they are**: a distrito spelled in a way that is not in
  the table passes unchanged.
- **Free text** is redacted word by word: Free Text Redaction splits on whitespace, so a phone
  written `8312 4567` is reached as two words and not as one number. The patterns of the set are the
  compact forms.
- **Children and adolescents**: a birth date moves at most 60 days, so few people cross their 18th
  birthday — but some do. Split the rule by age group with Multi-Column Condition where that matters.
- **The ten-year clock of art. 6.1 travels with the data**: masking a value does not reset the date
  of the facts it records.
- **The engine key** links masked values back to production for whoever holds both — the copy is
  *pseudonymized*, not *desasociada* in the sense of article 2 r) of the Reglamento, and still
  personal data for them. Keep the key apart from the copy.

## Checking

```bash
node presets/costarica-ley-8968/build.mjs
node presets/costarica-ley-8968/verify.mjs
node presets/_doc/build.mjs costarica-ley-8968
```

The last one regenerates the PDFs; run it after any change to the set or to `doc/`.

`verify.mjs` reviews every classifier configuration, profiles 146 columns with the whole set at its
threshold and again with the essential pack — well named, badly named and 30 columns that are not
personal data, among them a cédula jurídica — and audits the generalization for all 492 distritos,
84 cantones and 7 provincias, checking that every one of the 11,582 replacements names a place of at
least 20,000 and that no code leaves its provincia. It then masks the sample values, placeholders
and each algorithm's own sample input through the running app (`DLPX_URL`, default
`http://localhost:3000`), checking outputs against their vocabulary; 200 cédulas for distinctness,
shape and a provincia digit that is never zero; and 200 IBANs, one hundred compact and one hundred
printed in groups of four, for **validity under ISO 13616**, distinctness and the bank they keep.
Each value forks a JVM.
