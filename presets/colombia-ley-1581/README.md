# Colombia — Ley 1581 de 2012

A profile set that finds Colombian personal data in a database and masks it, built for
[Ley Estatutaria 1581 de 2012](http://www.secretariasenado.gov.co/senado/basedoc/ley_1581_2012.html)
on the protection of personal data, regulated by Decree 1377 of 2013 (compiled in Decree 1074 of 2015)
and enforced by the Superintendencia de Industria y Comercio. Personal data is information that can be
associated with a determined or determinable person (art. 3): data that can no longer be associated
with anyone is outside the law. The goal is a non-production copy that no longer identifies anyone:
not only the direct identifiers, but the quasi-identifiers that re-identify people in combination, the
sensitive data of art. 5 — racial or ethnic origin, political orientation, religious or philosophical
convictions, trade unions, social and human rights organizations, parties, health, sexual life and
biometric data —, victims of the armed conflict, financial data under Law 1266 of 2008 and criminal
records.

## Detection

Every domain has a **Path** classifier on the column name, in Spanish, in the abbreviations of
Colombian systems (`NRO_DOCUMENTO`, `CC_CLIENTE`, `FEC_NAC`, `COD_MPIO`, `DX_PRINCIPAL`, `ESTRATO`,
`GRUPO_SISBEN`) and in English. Names that are ambiguous on their own (`NOMBRE`, `DOCUMENTO`, `CARGO`,
`PROCESO`) carry a lower strength and need the values to agree; generic ones exclude what they are not
(`NOMBRE_PRODUCTO`, `RAZON_SOCIAL`, `ID_MENSAJE`).

Where the values have a recognizable shape or vocabulary, a **Regex** or **List** classifier finds the
data in columns whose name says nothing (`CAMPO1`, `OBS`): cédulas with dots, NITs, given names and
surnames, e-mail, Colombian mobiles and landlines, addresses in the street grid notation
(`Carrera 7 # 32-16`), cards (Luhn), IPs, MAC and IMEI, car and motorcycle plates, property registration
numbers, municipalities, ethnic self-recognition, indigenous peoples and languages, religions, parties,
trade unions, victimizing events, ICD-10 codes, medicines, blood groups, EPS and regimes, strata and
Sisbén groups.

**Type** classifiers rule domains out where the column cannot hold the data: an e-mail is text of at
least 5 characters, a birth date a date, timestamp or text, an amount a number.

## Masking

All algorithms are deterministic for the engine's key, so the same value masks the same way in
every table and every job. Placeholders such as `N/A` or `-` do not fail a row: where there is nothing
to mask they pass through.

### Cédula and NIT

The cédula has no check digit; it is masked digit by digit, dots kept, one-to-one — and the same number
masks the same way as identity card, NUIP, driving licence and military card. A document column that
mixes cédulas, NITs and passports is masked value by value by shape.

The NIT keeps a **valid DIAN check digit** (weights 3 7 13 … 71 from the right, modulus 11; remainder 0
or 1 is the digit itself). Check Digit writes 11 − r and gets remainder 1 wrong, so each NIT goes through
two steps: a Check Digit with doubled weights masks the body and writes an auxiliary digit that is 9
exactly when the true remainder is 1; a Regex Decompose turns that 9 into 1 or recomputes the digit
with the true weights. One pair per body length, 6 to 10 digits. The body is masked like the cédula, so a
natural person's NIT still matches their cédula.

### L3 — Sensitive data (art. 5)

Ethnic self-recognition (2018 census categories and codes), indigenous peoples, native languages, religion,
philosophical convictions, political orientation, party membership (the 28 organizations with legal
personality certified by the CNE), trade unions, social and human rights organizations, biometric and
genetic data, sexual orientation and sexual life, gender identity — and two categories that in Colombia
expose people to discrimination and violence: **victims of the armed conflict** (victimizing event of the
Registro Único de Víctimas, confidential under Law 1448 of 2011) and **people in reincorporation**.

Categorical columns keep their shape: a yes/no flag stays a flag in the same vocabulary, a numeric code is
replaced by another code of the same catalog, and any other value is replaced from a list of the category.
Where the values share no vocabulary — convictions, unions, social organizations, sexual life,
reincorporation — they are suppressed to `Sin información`. Biometric and genetic data become `X`.

### SALUD — Health data

Health identifiers, affiliation (regime and EPS), ICD-10 codes with or without the dot (`F331`, `I10X`),
CUPS procedures, medicines, clinical notes, test results, blood group and Rh (printed on the cédula),
disability, occupational health (fitness, FURAT, FUREL, sick leave), reproductive and mental health.

### FIN — Financial and socioeconomic data (Law 1266 of 2008)

Credit bureau scores and negative reports, debt, income, assets and pensions in pesos, the
**socioeconomic stratum** (replaced within its band: 1–2, 3–4, 5–6), the **Sisbén IV** group (letter kept,
subgroup replaced within the group), social programmes and housing tenure.

### PENAL — Criminal records and proceedings

Judicial, disciplinary and fiscal records and corrective measures replaced by a generic status, flags
kept; 23-digit Rama Judicial file numbers keep city, court, office and year and have the consecutive masked.

## Municipalities

The set carries the DANE 2026 population projection of the 1,122 DIVIPOLA municipalities and
non-municipalized areas and their location. 694 have fewer than 20,000 inhabitants (6.4 million people,
12%) and become the nearest municipality of their department with at least 20,000; every department has
one. A name shared by several municipalities — or differing only in accents — is generalized only when all
are small; written with the department (`La Unión - Sucre`, `La Unión (Sucre)`) it always is. Common
spellings (`Cartagena`, `Cúcuta`, `Mompox`, `Bogotá`) are recognized.

## Limits

- **Binary columns** (cédula photos with fingerprint, biometric selfies, clinical PDFs in BLOBs) cannot be
  masked by these algorithms. Inventory them and report them as residual risk.
- **NITs with a body under 6 or over 10 digits** are masked digit by digit without recomputing the check
  digit. A NIT with neither hyphen nor dots is read as body and digit.
- **A check digit in a column of its own** is masked, not recomputed.
- **Department, bank code and phone prefix are not masked.**
- **Data Cleansing leaves unknown values as they are**: a municipality spelled in a way that is not in the
  table passes unchanged.
- **Free text** is redacted token by token: a phone written with spaces is not found there.
- **Children and adolescents** (art. 7): a birth date moves at most 60 days, so few people cross their 14th
  or 18th birthday — but some do. Split the rule by age group with Multi-Column Condition where that matters.
- **The engine key** links masked values back to production for whoever holds both — pseudonymized data,
  still personal data; keep it away from the users of the masked copy.

## Checking

```bash
node presets/colombia-ley-1581/build.mjs
node presets/colombia-ley-1581/verify.mjs
node presets/_doc/build.mjs colombia-ley-1581
```

The last one regenerates the PDFs; run it after any change to the set or to `doc/`.

`verify.mjs` reviews every classifier configuration, profiles 151 columns with the whole set at its
threshold and again with the essential pack — well named, badly named and 31 columns that are not personal
data — and audits the generalization table for all 1,122 municipalities. It then masks the sample values,
placeholders and each algorithm's own sample input through the running app (`DLPX_URL`, default
`http://localhost:3000`), checking outputs against their vocabulary; 200 NITs of every length for validity
and collisions, remainder 1 included; 150 cédulas for collisions; and 20 people whose cédula and NIT must
still agree. Each value forks a JVM.
