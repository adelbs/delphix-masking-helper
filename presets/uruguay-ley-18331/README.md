# Uruguay — Ley 18.331 de Protección de Datos Personales

A profile set that finds Uruguayan personal data in a database and masks it, built for the
[Ley 18.331 de Protección de Datos Personales y Acción de Habeas Data](https://www.impo.com.uy/bases/leyes/18331-2008)
(11 August 2008), regulated by Decree 414/009 and amended by Ley 18.719 (2010), Ley 18.996 (2012),
Ley 19.355 (2015), **Ley 19.670 (2018)** — extraterritorial reach, breach notification, data
protection officer — and **Ley 19.924 (2020)** — the biometric datum —, with those additions
regulated by [Decree 64/020](https://www.impo.com.uy/bases/decretos/64-2020). The authority is the
Unidad Reguladora y de Control de Datos Personales (URCDP), inside AGESIC; Uruguay is one of only
two Latin American countries the European Union has recognized as providing an adequate level of
protection.

Two things make this set different from its neighbours. **Article 4 D) makes a legal person a data
subject**: personal data is information about determinate or determinable *natural or legal*
persons, so the razón social, the nombre de fantasía and a company RUT are personal data here.
And **article 17 D)** is the clause the whole project rests on: the consent of the data subject is
not needed to communicate data when "se hubiera aplicado un procedimiento de disociación de la
información, de modo que los titulares de los datos no sean identificables" — while **article 8 a)
of Decree 64/020** lists, first among the privacy-by-design measures, the "técnicas de disociación,
seudonimización y minimización de datos".

## Detection

Every domain has a **Path** classifier on the column name, in Spanish, in the abbreviations of
Uruguayan systems (`CI`, `FEC_NAC`, `RUT`, `BPS`, `IUE`, `PADRON`) and in English. Names that are
ambiguous on their own (`NOMBRE`, `DOCUMENTO`, `EXPEDIENTE`, `CARGO`) carry a lower strength and need
the values to agree; generic ones exclude what they are not (`NOMBRE_PRODUCTO`, `ID_MENSAJE`, and
`RUTA`, which must not read as `RUT`).

Where the values have a recognizable shape or vocabulary, a **Regex** or **List** classifier finds
the data in columns whose name says nothing (`CAMPO1`, `OBS`): cédulas written `1.234.567-2`,
twelve-digit RUTs, mobiles starting 09, addresses with a door number and an apartamento, cards
(Luhn), IPs, MAC and IMEI, plates, localidades, ascendencia categories, indigenous peoples,
religions, parties, ICD-10 codes, medicines, blood groups and health cover.

**Type** classifiers rule domains out where the column cannot hold the data: an e-mail is text of at
least 5 characters, a birth date a date, timestamp or text, an amount a number.

## Masking

All algorithms are deterministic for the engine's key, so the same value masks the same way in every
table and every job. Placeholders such as `N/A` or `-` do not fail a row: where there is nothing to
mask they pass through.

### Cédula and RUT

The **cédula de identidad** is the simple case: seven digits with the weights 2 9 8 7 6 3 4 from the
left, modulus 10, and the digit is ten minus the remainder — exactly what Check Digit computes, so
one step masks the seven digits and writes the eighth. The dots and the hyphen of `1.234.567-2` pass
through untouched.

The **RUT** is twelve digits — two for the kind of taxpayer, six for the number, three for the
branch and one check digit — with the digit computed modulus 11 over the weights 4 3 2 9 8 7 6 5 4 3
2, eleven minus the remainder, written **0 when that is eleven and 1 when it is ten**. Check Digit
writes *modulus minus remainder* and writes ten as `0`, so it agrees everywhere except on one
remainder: with a remainder of 1 the true digit is 1 and it writes 0, and a remainder of 0 — whose
true digit really is 0 — produces the same written zero. The set tells the two apart with an
**auxiliary Check Digit whose weights are the real ones doubled, modulo 11**: doubling maps 0 to 0
and 1 to 2, so the auxiliary writes `0` for the first and `9` for the second. A Regex Decompose then
reads the pair, writes the true digit and drops the auxiliary — four steps, no lookup table. The kind
of taxpayer and the branch stay where they were. Uruguay's RUT is not derived from the cédula, so
the two are masked independently.

### L3 — Sensitive data (art. 4 E and art. 4 Ñ)

Ascendencia étnico-racial, indigenous people, nationality, religion — Uruguay is the most secular
country of the region, so a declared one says a great deal —, **convicciones morales** (named in art.
4 E beside the religious ones), political preferences and party membership, trade union membership,
**biometric data** (art. 4 Ñ, 2020, which art. 18-BIS puts an impact assessment in front of), genetic
data, sexual orientation, **vida sexual** and gender identity, and victims of violence (Ley 19.580).

Categorical columns keep their shape: a yes/no flag stays a flag in the same vocabulary, a numeric
code is replaced by another code, and any other value is replaced from a list of the category. Where
the values share no vocabulary — convictions, unions, sexual life, victims — they are suppressed to
`Sin información`. Biometric and genetic data become `X`.

### SALUD — Health data (art. 4 E, art. 17 C and art. 19)

Art. 17 C) allows health data to be communicated for sanitary or epidemiological reasons only while
"preservando la identidad de los titulares de los datos mediante mecanismos de disociación
adecuados", which is what this group is for: clinical record numbers, cover (ASSE, mutualista/IAMC,
private, military and police health), ICD-10 codes with or without the dot, procedures, medicines,
clinical notes, test results, **HIV and STIs** in a domain of their own, blood group, disability, the
compulsory **carné de salud**, reproductive and mental health.

### FIN — Commercial and credit data (art. 22)

The *actividad comercial o crediticia* of art. 22, with the Clearing de Informes and the Banco
Central debtor categories 1C to 5; debt, income, assets and pasividades in pesos uruguayos; social
programmes, the MIDES Índice de Carencias Críticas and housing tenure. Article 22 caps the
registration of a natural person's commercial obligation at **five years**, renewable once.

### PENAL — Offences (art. 18)

The final paragraph of art. 18 reserves the data on **criminal, civil or administrative** offences to
the competent public authorities: a private company that copies such a column into a test
environment is processing data it may not process at all. Record certificates and procedural
situation replaced by a generic status, flags kept; the IUE keeps the year and has the office and the
correlative masked.

## Geography

The set carries the population the 2023 census counted for the 652 localidades censales and the 19
departamentos. **624 of the localidades have fewer than 20,000 inhabitants** (873,000 people, a
quarter of the country) and become the nearest localidad of at least 20,000 in the same
departamento. Every departamento has one, so a generalized value never leaves its departamento, and
the 19 departamentos stay — the smallest, Flores, has some 24,600 inhabitants. A name shared by
several localidades — Santa Lucía, Cerro Chato, Tala — is generalized only when all of them are
small, and then follows the most populous. Written with the departamento (`Piriápolis - Maldonado`)
the same rule applies inside it. A name that is also a **departamento** is left alone so that a
departamento column is never rewritten; every localidad in that position is itself above the
threshold.

## Limits

- **Binary columns** (cédula photos, fingerprints, clinical PDFs in BLOBs) cannot be masked by these
  algorithms. Inventory them and report them as residual risk.
- **The location of 186 of the 652 localidades is approximate** — the centre of the located
  localidades of their departamento. Because seventeen departamentos have a single target, this
  changes the outcome for only 84 small localidades, all in Canelones, Maldonado and San José, and
  the guarantee of at least 20,000 inhabitants in the same departamento holds in every case.
- **The INE publishes the localidad names in capitals without their accents.** The set restores them
  with a dictionary and writes both spellings into the tables, but a name it could not restore comes
  out unaccented.
- **Departamento, bank code and phone prefix are not masked.**
- **Data Cleansing leaves unknown values as they are**: a localidad spelled in a way that is not in
  the table passes unchanged.
- **Free text** is redacted token by token: a phone written with spaces is not always found.
- **Masked bitcoin addresses** no longer pass their own checksum — deliberately.
- **Children and adolescents**: a birth date moves at most 60 days, so few people cross their 18th
  birthday — but some do. Split the rule by age group with Multi-Column Condition where that matters.
- **The five-year clock of art. 22 travels with the data**: masking the amount of a debt does not
  reset the date on which it became due.
- **The engine key** links masked values back to production for whoever holds both — the copy is
  *pseudonymized* in the sense of art. 8 a) of Decree 64/020, not dissociated in the sense of art.
  4 G), and still personal data for them. Keep the key apart from the copy.

## Checking

```bash
node presets/uruguay-ley-18331/build.mjs
node presets/uruguay-ley-18331/verify.mjs
node presets/_doc/build.mjs uruguay-ley-18331
```

The last one regenerates the PDFs; run it after any change to the set or to `doc/`.

`verify.mjs` reviews every classifier configuration, profiles 144 columns with the whole set at its
threshold and again with the essential pack — well named, badly named and 29 columns that are not
personal data — and audits the generalization table for all 652 localidades and 19 departamentos,
checking that no INE code leaves its departamento. It then masks the sample values, placeholders and
each algorithm's own sample input through the running app (`DLPX_URL`, default
`http://localhost:3000`), checking outputs against their vocabulary; 200 cédulas for validity,
distinctness and the dots and hyphen they keep, and 200 RUTs for validity, distinctness and the kind
of taxpayer and branch they keep. Each value forks a JVM.
