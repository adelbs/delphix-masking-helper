# Peru — Ley 29733 de Protección de Datos Personales

A profile set that finds Peruvian personal data in a database and masks it, built for the
[Ley 29733 de Protección de Datos Personales](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/minjus/normas-legales/249437-29733)
(3 July 2011) and its regulation, [Decreto Supremo 016-2024-JUS](https://busquedas.elperuano.pe/dispositivo/NL/2347978-1)
(published 30 November 2024, in force since 30 March 2025), applied and supervised by the Autoridad
Nacional de Protección de Datos Personales (ANPD) of the Ministry of Justice and Human Rights.
Personal data is information about a natural person that identifies them or makes them identifiable
through means that can reasonably be used (art. 2, 4); **anonymization** is irreversible (art. 2, 14)
and **dissociation** is, in the words of art. 41.1 of the regulation, a procedure that "is
reversible". The goal is a non-production copy that no longer identifies anyone: not only the direct
identifiers, but the quasi-identifiers that re-identify people in combination, the sensitive data of
art. 2, 5 — biometric data that identifies on its own, racial and ethnic origin, **economic income**,
political, religious, philosophical and moral opinions or convictions, trade union membership, and
health or sexual life —, plus genetic data, gender identity, indigenous people and language,
financial data and criminal records.

## Detection

Every domain has a **Path** classifier on the column name, in Spanish, in the abbreviations of
Peruvian systems (`NUM_DOC`, `FEC_NAC`, `UBIGEO`, `RUC`, `ESSALUD`, `CODIGO_MODULAR`) and in English.
Names that are ambiguous on their own (`NOMBRE`, `DOCUMENTO`, `EXPEDIENTE`, `CARGO`) carry a lower
strength and need the values to agree; generic ones exclude what they are not (`NOMBRE_PRODUCTO`,
`ID_MENSAJE`, `DISTRITO_JUDICIAL`).

Where the values have a recognizable shape or vocabulary, a **Regex** or **List** classifier finds
the data in columns whose name says nothing (`CAMPO1`, `OBS`): DNIs, eleven-digit RUCs, nine-digit
mobiles, addresses with urbanización, manzana and lote, cards (Luhn), IPs, MAC and IMEI, plates,
distritos, ethnic groups, indigenous peoples and languages, religions, parties, ICD-10 codes,
medicines, blood groups and health cover.

**Type** classifiers rule domains out where the column cannot hold the data: an e-mail is text of at
least 5 characters, a birth date a date, timestamp or text, an amount a number.

## Masking

All algorithms are deterministic for the engine's key, so the same value masks the same way in every
table and every job. Placeholders such as `N/A` or `-` do not fail a row: where there is nothing to
mask they pass through.

### DNI and RUC

The **DNI** is eight digits plus a verification character: RENIEC computes it modulus 11 over the
weights 3 2 7 6 5 4 3 2, and eleven minus the remainder (zero stays zero) indexes two series — the
digits 6 7 8 9 0 1 1 2 3 4 5 and the letters K A B C D E F G H I J, the letters belonging to the
documents issued up to 14 August 2007.

The **RUC** is eleven digits: two for the kind of taxpayer — 10 and 15 for a natural person, 17 for
an estate, 20 for a legal person —, eight for the number and one check digit, modulus 11 over the
weights 5 4 3 2 7 6 5 4 3 2, eleven minus the remainder, written 0 when that is ten and 1 when it is
eleven. For a natural person with a 10, those eight digits **are the DNI**.

Check Digit writes *modulus minus remainder* and writes ten as `0`, so both rules agree with it
except where that written zero is ambiguous: it stands for a remainder of 0 and for a remainder of 1
at once, and the two need different digits — 6 and 5 for the DNI, 1 and 0 for the RUC. The set
resolves it with an **auxiliary Check Digit whose weights are the real ones doubled, modulo 11**:
doubling maps 0 to 0 and 1 to 2, so the auxiliary writes `0` for the first and `9` for the second. A
Regex Decompose then reads the pair, writes the true digit and drops the auxiliary — four steps, no
lookup table. Because the RUC keeps its two leading digits and maps the other eight exactly as the
DNI does, the masked DNI and the masked RUC of the same person still agree.

### L3 — Sensitive data (art. 2, 5)

Biometric data that identifies on its own, racial and ethnic origin (2017 census categories),
indigenous people and language, nationality, religion, philosophical and moral convictions, political
opinion and party membership, trade union membership, genetic data, sexual orientation and sexual
life, gender identity, and victims of the 1980–2000 violence (Ley 28592) or of violence against women
(Ley 30364). **Economic income**, which art. 2, 5 also lists, lives in the FIN group below.

Categorical columns keep their shape: a yes/no flag stays a flag in the same vocabulary, a numeric
code is replaced by another code, and any other value is replaced from a list of the category. Where
the values share no vocabulary — convictions, unions, sexual life, victims — they are suppressed to
`Sin información`. Biometric and genetic data become `X`.

### SALUD — Health data (art. 2, 5)

Clinical record numbers, cover (EsSalud, SIS, EPS, sanidad PNP and FFAA, private), ICD-10 codes with
or without the dot, procedures, medicines, clinical notes, test results, **HIV and STIs** in a domain
of their own, blood group, disability (CONADIS), occupational, reproductive and mental health.

### FIN — Financial and socioeconomic data

**Income is sensitive data in Peru** (art. 2, 5) — no neighbouring law says so. Beside it: credit
history and the SBS classification of the debtor (Ley 27489), debt, assets and pensions in soles,
social programmes, the SISFOH socioeconomic classification and housing tenure.

### PENAL — Criminal records

Record certificates and procedural situation replaced by a generic status, flags kept; court and
prosecution file numbers keep the year, the judicial district and the specialty and have the
correlative masked.

## Geography

The set carries the population the 2017 census counted and the location the INEI publishes for the
1,892 distritos and the 196 provincias. Peru's distritos are small: **1,633 of them have fewer than
20,000 inhabitants** (7.4 million people) and become the nearest distrito of their provincia with at
least 20,000 — of their departamento when the provincia has none. The 33 provincias under 20,000 —
Purús has 2,860 — follow the same rule inside their departamento. The 25 departamentos stay. A name
shared by several places — San Juan, Santa Rosa, San José — is generalized only when all of them are
small, and then follows the most populous; when that bearer is a provincia, the replacement is a
provincia. Written with the provincia or the departamento (`Balsas - Amazonas`) the same rule applies
inside it.

## Limits

- **Binary columns** (DNI photos, fingerprints, clinical PDFs in BLOBs) cannot be masked by these
  algorithms. Inventory them and report them as residual risk.
- **A verification character in a column of its own** is masked, not recomputed.
- **Departamento, bank code and phone area code are not masked.**
- **Data Cleansing leaves unknown values as they are**: a distrito spelled in a way that is not in the
  table passes unchanged.
- **Free text** is redacted token by token: a phone written with spaces is not always found.
- **Masked bitcoin addresses** no longer pass their own checksum — deliberately.
- **Children and adolescents**: a birth date moves at most 60 days, so few people cross their 14th or
  18th birthday — but some do, and art. 25 of the regulation makes that line matter. Split the rule
  by age group with Multi-Column Condition where it does.
- **The engine key** links masked values back to production for whoever holds both — the copy is
  *dissociated* in the sense of art. 41 of the regulation, a procedure the regulation itself calls
  reversible, not anonymized, and still personal data.

## Checking

```bash
node presets/peru-ley-29733/build.mjs
node presets/peru-ley-29733/verify.mjs
node presets/_doc/build.mjs peru-ley-29733
```

The last one regenerates the PDFs; run it after any change to the set or to `doc/`.

`verify.mjs` reviews every classifier configuration, profiles 145 columns with the whole set at its
threshold and again with the essential pack — well named, badly named and 29 columns that are not
personal data — and audits the generalization table for all 1,892 distritos and 196 provincias. It
then masks the sample values, placeholders and each algorithm's own sample input through the running
app (`DLPX_URL`, default `http://localhost:3000`), checking outputs against their vocabulary; 150
DNIs of the digit series and 60 of the letter series, 200 RUCs of the four kinds of taxpayer for
validity and collisions, and 20 DNI and RUC pairs to confirm they still agree. Each value forks a JVM.
